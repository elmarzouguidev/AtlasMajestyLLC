Technical SEO: Build a Website Search Engines Can Fully Understand

Technical SEO ensures the foundations of your website are solid — so search engines can crawl, index, and rank your content effectively.

Get a Technical SEO Audit → Talk to a Technical SEO Specialist
Why Technical SEO Matters

Great Content Doesn't Rank If Search Engines Can't Properly Access It

Technical SEO addresses the behind-the-scenes factors that influence how effectively Google can discover, crawl, understand, and index your website — and ultimately how well it ranks in search results.

🕷️

Crawlability

Ensure Googlebot can reach and access every important page on your website without being blocked by technical errors, incorrect robots.txt settings, or broken redirects.

📋

Indexation

Verify that your important pages are indexed by Google and that low-value or duplicate pages are properly excluded from the index using canonical tags and noindex directives.

⚡

Core Web Vitals

Google uses page experience signals — including Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS), and Interaction to Next Paint (INP) — as ranking factors.

🏗️

Site Architecture

A clear, logical URL structure and information hierarchy helps Google understand how your pages relate to each other and which ones are most important.

🔗

Structured Data

Schema.org markup helps Google understand the specific type of content on each page — enabling rich results and improving how your pages appear in search results.

📱

Mobile Usability

Google indexes websites primarily based on their mobile version. A fully responsive, mobile-optimized website is a technical SEO necessity.

Core Web Vitals

Page Experience Signals That Influence Your Rankings

Google uses Core Web Vitals as a ranking factor, measuring the real-world experience users have when they visit your website. Poor scores can limit your ability to rank even if your content is excellent.

We identify and address the specific technical issues causing poor Core Web Vitals scores on your website.

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) — how quickly the main content loads
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) — how stable the page layout is as it loads
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint) — how responsive the page is to user input
  • Image optimization and lazy loading
  • Render-blocking resource elimination
  • Server response time improvements
